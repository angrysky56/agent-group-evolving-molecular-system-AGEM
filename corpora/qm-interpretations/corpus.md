## The stance axes

Nine binary or near-binary commitments. An interpretation is a point in this space.

1. **Wavefunction status** — ψ-ontic (ψ is a physical property of the system) vs
   ψ-epistemic (ψ encodes an agent's information about an underlying state λ).
2. **Collapse** — a real dynamical process vs no collapse at all.
3. **Outcomes** — exactly one outcome occurs vs all outcomes occur on separate branches.
4. **Determinism** — the fundamental dynamics is deterministic vs irreducibly stochastic.
5. **Locality** — no influence propagates faster than light vs nonlocal influence.
6. **Hidden variables** — the state description is incomplete, there are further
   variables λ, vs ψ is complete.
7. **Noncontextuality** — an observable's value is independent of which other
   compatible observables are measured alongside it, vs values are contextual.
8. **Measurement independence** (statistical independence, "free choice") — the
   settings chosen by experimenters are uncorrelated with the hidden state, vs
   they are correlated.
9. **Observer-independence** — measurement outcomes are absolute facts for all
   agents, vs facts are relative to an observer or agent.

## The positions

### Copenhagen (orthodox)

The Copenhagen position holds that ψ is not a picture of a physical thing but a
computational device for predicting measurement statistics, that measurement
induces a real collapse, that exactly one outcome occurs, and that the theory is
irreducibly indeterministic. It denies hidden variables: ψ is complete. It draws
a cut between quantum system and classical apparatus and declines to place the
apparatus inside the theory.

### Everettian / Many-Worlds

The Everettian position holds that ψ is ontic, that there is no collapse, that
the unitary dynamics is the whole story and therefore deterministic, and that all
outcomes occur on decoherent branches. It denies that exactly one outcome occurs.
Everettians claim locality is preserved precisely because nothing nonlocal need
happen when nothing is selected.

### de Broglie–Bohm (pilot wave)

The Bohmian position holds that ψ is ontic and guides particles that always have
definite positions, so hidden variables exist and the dynamics is deterministic
and single-outcome. It accepts explicit nonlocality: the guiding equation depends
instantaneously on the configuration of distant particles. It accepts
contextuality: measured values depend on the full experimental arrangement.

### GRW / CSL (objective collapse)

The objective-collapse position holds that ψ is ontic and that collapse is a real,
spontaneous, observer-independent physical process with a definite rate. Outcomes
are single and the dynamics is irreducibly stochastic. It denies hidden variables.
It accepts nonlocality. Crucially it makes predictions that differ from unmodified
quantum mechanics at large mass scales, so it is empirically distinguishable.

### QBism

The QBist position holds that ψ is epistemic in a radically personalist sense — a
single agent's degrees of belief — that "collapse" is nothing but that agent's
Bayesian update, and that measurement outcomes are experiences private to the
agent who elicits them. It denies observer-independent facts. It denies hidden
variables in the usual sense: there is no λ that the agent is ignorant about.

### Relational QM

The relational position holds that states and outcomes are relative to a physical
system, that there is no observer-independent fact about which outcome occurred,
and that ψ describes a relation rather than an absolute property. It denies
observer-independence while asserting locality.

### Superdeterminism

The superdeterministic position holds that hidden variables exist, that the
dynamics is deterministic and local, and that the correlations Bell derived are
explained by denying measurement independence: the experimenters' settings are
themselves correlated with the hidden state.

### Consistent Histories

The consistent-histories position holds that there is no collapse, that
probabilities attach to whole histories within a chosen framework, and that
statements are only meaningful relative to a framework. It denies that there is a
single framework-independent description.

### Transactional

The transactional position holds that ψ is ontic and that a measurement is a
handshake between retarded and advanced waves, accepting retrocausal influence in
order to preserve locality in the ordinary spacelike sense.

## The impossibilities

The corpus asserts these, on the authority of published theorems.

### Bell

No position can hold **all** of: locality, hidden variables assigning definite
pre-existing outcomes, measurement independence, and empirical adequacy to
quantum predictions. The experimental violation of Bell inequalities makes this a
live constraint, not a formal curiosity. Each surviving interpretation gives up
exactly one conjunct: Bohm gives up locality, Copenhagen and QBism give up
definite pre-existing values, superdeterminism gives up measurement independence,
Everett gives up single outcomes.

### Kochen–Specker

No position can hold all of: noncontextual value assignment to all observables,
value-definiteness for all observables, and empirical adequacy, in Hilbert spaces
of dimension three or higher. Any hidden-variable theory must therefore be
contextual.

### Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph

No position can hold all of: ψ-epistemic (distinct pure states have overlapping
distributions over λ), preparation independence (independently prepared systems
have independent ontic states), and empirical adequacy. Note that preparation
independence is itself contested — Gao has argued the theorem does not require it —
so this impossibility is asserted with a live objection attached.

### Frauchiger–Renner

No position can hold all three of: universal applicability of quantum theory
including to agents who themselves use it (Q), self-consistency of the conclusions
different agents reach (C), and single outcomes (S). Everett gives up S; QBism and
relational QM give up C in its absolute form; Copenhagen gives up Q by refusing to
put the observer inside the theory.

### Leggett–Garg

No position can hold all of: macrorealism (a macroscopic system is at all times in
one of its available definite states), noninvasive measurability (that state can
in principle be determined without disturbing the subsequent dynamics), and
empirical adequacy.

### Brukner / Wigner's friend

No position can hold all of: observer-independent facts, locality, free choice,
and universal applicability of quantum theory to observers.
