## Field 1 — is consciousness quantum-mechanically constituted?

**The Orch-OR position** (Penrose, Hameroff) holds that consciousness arises from
orchestrated objective reduction of quantum states in microtubules, that this
requires sustained quantum coherence in neuronal cytoskeletal structures on
timescales relevant to neural processing (tens to hundreds of milliseconds), and
that consciousness is therefore non-computable in the Turing sense.

**The decoherence objection** (Tegmark) holds that decoherence times for
microtubule-scale superpositions in a warm, wet, ionic brain are on the order of
10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ seconds — ten or more orders of magnitude too short for any
neurally relevant process. Hagan, Hameroff and Tuszyński dispute the calculation
and obtain longer times; the dispute is over the model, not the principle.

**The classical-sufficiency position** holds that neural computation is
adequately described classically, that quantum effects are thermal noise at the
relevant scales, and that consciousness supervenes on classically-describable
neural dynamics.

## Field 2 — is quantum biology real?

**The established-effects position** holds that non-trivial quantum effects are
demonstrated in biology: radical-pair magnetoreception in avian navigation,
which requires spin coherence surviving microseconds in a warm protein; and
enzymatic hydrogen tunnelling, which is uncontroversial.

**The photosynthesis-retraction position** holds that the long-lived electronic
coherence originally reported in photosynthetic light-harvesting complexes has
been substantially reinterpreted — the oscillations are now widely attributed to
vibrational rather than electronic coherence, and the functional role is
contested.

The corpus notes the asymmetry: demonstrated quantum effects in biology involve
*specific, protected, short-lived* coherences serving *specific* functions. None
demonstrates the sustained, large-scale, integrated coherence Orch-OR requires.

## Field 3 — what was the genetic code optimized for?

**The error-minimization position** (Freeland, Hurst) holds that the standard
genetic code is extraordinarily good at minimizing the phenotypic impact of point
mutations and mistranslations — better than the overwhelming majority of randomly
generated alternative codes — and that this is evidence of selection for
robustness.

**The stereochemical position** holds that codon–amino-acid assignments reflect
direct physicochemical affinity between triplets and residues.

**The coevolution position** holds that the code expanded alongside amino acid
biosynthetic pathways, and that the pattern reflects metabolic history rather
than optimization.

**The frozen-accident position** (Crick) holds that assignments were largely
arbitrary and became fixed because any change would be catastrophically
disruptive, so present structure is history, not optimization.

## The bridging claims under test

**Teleological bridge.** The strong synthesis asserts that the genetic code was
optimized *for* consciousness — that consciousness is among the targets of the
selection process that shaped the code.

**Enabling bridge.** The weak synthesis asserts only that the code was optimized
for error-robustness, that error-robustness permits open-ended complexity, and
that consciousness is a downstream consequence of that complexity — selection
*of* consciousness, not selection *for* it.

**Substrate bridge.** The synthesis in either form asserts that consciousness
depends on the specific quantum-mechanical substrate that biological molecules
instantiate.

## The constraints the corpus imports

**Selection-for versus selection-of.** Evolutionary theory holds that a trait is
selected *for* only if it is causally responsible for differential reproductive
success at the time of selection. A trait that appears hundreds of millions of
years after the selection event cannot be among that event's targets.

**Substrate independence.** The multiple-realizability position, imported from
the theories-of-mind corpus, holds that a mental kind can be realized in
physically heterogeneous substrates. The substrate bridge denies this.

**Computability.** The Church–Turing-adequacy position holds that any physically
realizable process can be simulated to arbitrary accuracy by a Turing machine.
Orch-OR's non-computability claim denies this for the specific case of objective
reduction.
