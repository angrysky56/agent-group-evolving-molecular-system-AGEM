# The Origin of the Genetic Code

## Frozen accident

There are codons, there are amino acids, and there is an assignment relating
them. The assignment found in extant organisms is very nearly universal across
all three domains of life.

The allocation of particular codons to particular amino acids is arbitrary. No
stereochemical affinity between a codon and the amino acid it specifies dictated
which triplet was allocated to which residue. The assignment could have come out
otherwise, and in a rerun of early evolution it would have.

What holds the assignment fixed is not its quality but the cost of altering it.
Once a substantial number of proteins are being synthesised through a given
assignment, any change to that assignment changes every protein in the organism
at once. Such a change is overwhelmingly likely to be lethal. The assignment is
therefore frozen: it persists because it cannot be modified, not because it is
good.

Universality is a consequence of the freezing, not evidence of optimality. A
frozen assignment shared by descent will look universal whether or not it was
ever selected for any property it has. The code is a historical accident that
became unalterable.

The earliest code need not have covered all twenty amino acids. A primitive
system using a few amino acids could have expanded as further amino acids became
available. But the assignments made during that expansion were themselves not
determined by any property of the triplets or the residues involved.

## Stereochemical affinity

There are direct physicochemical interactions between free amino acids and short
RNA sequences. RNA aptamers selected in vitro for binding to a particular amino
acid contain that amino acid's cognate codons, or its anticodons, within or
immediately adjacent to the binding site at frequencies higher than chance
predicts. This has been reported for arginine, isoleucine, tryptophan, tyrosine,
phenylalanine, histidine and leucine among others, and the combined probability
of the observed enrichment arising by chance across the tested set is very small.

The assignment is therefore not arbitrary. It records a real chemical affinity
between a triplet and the residue it came to specify. The triplets that now
function as codons and anticodons were once part of amino acid binding sites in
RNA, and they escaped those sites into a templating role. What is now a
conventional relation began as a physical one.

A code seeded this way does not require that every assignment be affinity-driven.
Affinity fixes an initial set of assignments; subsequent additions may follow by
other routes. But the core of the assignment is grounded in chemistry that can
still be measured today, and the measurement is repeatable in vitro without any
biological machinery present.

Amino acid polarity correlates with codon composition. This correlation is
physical in origin, not the residue of a selective process.

## Coevolution with amino acid biosynthesis

The set of amino acids available to the earliest translation system was small.
The remaining amino acids arose later, by biosynthetic modification of amino
acids already present. Glutamine is made from glutamate; asparagine from
aspartate; tyrosine from phenylalanine; cysteine and tryptophan from serine;
isoleucine, methionine, threonine and lysine from aspartate; proline and arginine
from glutamate.

The structure of the code records that expansion. When a product amino acid
became available, it took over a portion of the codon domain formerly read as its
biosynthetic precursor. Precursor and product therefore occupy adjacent regions
of codon space, and they do so because of the pathway that connects them.

This predicts the observed pattern: codon domains of biosynthetically related
amino acids are contiguous far more often than a random assignment would produce.
The code is a fossil of metabolic history.

The assignment is not arbitrary, and it was not set by stereochemical affinity
between triplet and residue. Where a correlation with physicochemical similarity
exists, it follows from the fact that biosynthetically related amino acids tend
to be chemically similar. The apparent error tolerance of the code is a
consequence of the expansion mechanism rather than a target of selection.

## Adaptive error minimisation

Mistranslation and point mutation are unavoidable. An assignment in which
chemically similar amino acids occupy neighbouring codons converts many such
errors into conservative substitutions, whose effect on protein function is
small. An assignment without that property converts the same errors into radical
substitutions.

The standard code has that property to a marked degree. Amino acids sharing
similar polar requirement cluster in codon space; the second codon position in
particular tracks hydrophobicity. Assigning an error value to a code — the mean
change in amino acid property caused by a single-base substitution, weighted by
how often each substitution actually occurs — makes the comparison quantitative.

Haig and Hurst compared the standard code against randomly generated alternative
codes and reported that it outperformed all but roughly one in ten thousand.
Freeland and Hurst repeated the comparison with mistranslation weighted by
transition/transversion bias and by codon position, and reported that the
standard code outperformed all but approximately one in a million.

A code this far out in the tail of the distribution of possible codes was
selected for that property. The assignment is neither arbitrary nor frozen at the
point it happened to reach: it was optimised, and the optimisation is visible in
the statistic.

## Under-determination

Each of the standing accounts explains part of the structure of the code, and
none of them explains all of it.

The error-minimisation signal is real and it is strong, but its cause is not
settled. A code that minimises error is what direct selection on the assignment
would produce. It is also what an expansion driven by biosynthetic relatedness
would produce, since biosynthetically related amino acids are chemically similar.
It is also compatible with a stereochemically seeded core, since chemically
similar amino acids may bind similar triplets. The statistic does not discriminate
between these, and no other available evidence does either.

The aptamer enrichment is real for the amino acids where it has been measured,
but it covers a minority of the twenty and does not by itself account for the
global organisation of codon space. The biosynthetic correlations are real but
admit alternative groupings of the pathways. The frozen-accident account explains
universality without explaining structure, and every account must invoke freezing
at some point to explain why the assignment stopped changing.

The origin of the assignment is under-determined by the evidence now available.
This is a claim about the evidence, not a claim that the question has no answer.

## Index: structure of the standard codon table

Sixty-four triplets encode twenty amino acids and three stop signals. The
assignment is degenerate: most amino acids have more than one codon, and
synonymous codons overwhelmingly share their first two bases and differ in the
third. Third-position degeneracy corresponds to wobble in codon–anticodon pairing.

The second position is the strongest determinant of the amino acid's
physicochemical class. Every codon with uracil in the second position specifies a
hydrophobic amino acid: phenylalanine, leucine, isoleucine, methionine, valine.

Amino acids with similar polar requirement occupy neighbouring positions in codon
space more often than a random assignment predicts.

## Index: error value of the standard code against random alternatives

The error value of a code is computable. Generate alternative codes by permuting
amino acid assignments across the existing block structure, score each by the
mean property change caused by single-base substitutions, and rank the standard
code within that distribution.

The reported rank depends on the weighting model. Unweighted comparison places
the standard code better than roughly one in ten thousand random codes. Weighting
substitutions by transition/transversion bias and by codon position places it
better than approximately one in a million. The statistic is sensitive to the
choice of amino acid property, most often polar requirement.

## Index: aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase class split

The twenty aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases fall into two classes of ten. The two
classes share no structural homology in their catalytic domains. Class I enzymes
approach the tRNA acceptor stem from the minor groove and aminoacylate the 2'-OH;
Class II enzymes approach from the major groove and aminoacylate the 3'-OH.

The class split correlates with position in codon space and with amino acid
size and polarity, but the correlation is partial and admits exceptions. The
catalytic domains of the two classes have been reported to be encodable on
complementary strands of a single ancestral gene.

## Index: biosynthetic precursor–product graph

The twenty amino acids form a directed graph under the relation "is a
biosynthetic precursor of". Glutamate is a precursor of glutamine, proline and
arginine. Aspartate is a precursor of asparagine, threonine, methionine, lysine
and isoleucine. Phenylalanine is a precursor of tyrosine. Serine is a precursor
of cysteine and tryptophan. Pyruvate-derived and shikimate-derived families group
further amino acids.

The graph is independent of the code: it is established by metabolic biochemistry
without reference to codon assignments.

## Index: aptamer binding frequencies

RNA aptamers selected in vitro for binding a specified free amino acid can be
sequenced and their binding sites identified. The frequency of the amino acid's
cognate codon and anticodon triplets within those sites can be compared against
the frequency expected from base composition alone.

Enrichment has been reported for a subset of the twenty amino acids. The
measurement requires no ribosome, no synthetase, and no cell; it is a property of
RNA and free amino acids in solution. Amino acids for which sufficient aptamer
data do not exist are untested rather than negative.
